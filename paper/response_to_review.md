Every enumerated comment in the report has been addressed in the revised manuscript (attached; exactly 4 pages including references, the IEEE CAL limit), and the two issues raised in the overall assessment are handled as described under T1 and under "Hybrid baseline" below. All numbers quoted here were recomputed directly from the archived per-condition run artifacts (five multiseed Phase-4 tables, five bounds tables, six sweep tables, the Phase-1 profile, and the regenerated Phase-2 traces and Phase-3 placements); nothing is estimated from table roundings, and an independent second pass re-derived every figure in the paper from those files. ## Part 1 — Technical comments

**T1 (the "below the digital reference" claim rests on a self-handicapped reference).** Accepted, option (i). The paper is re-baselined. The pretrained digital model is now the stated deployment alternative; we measured it on exactly the same WikiText-103 test windows (279 windows, 285,417 predicted tokens): PPL 29.01, NLL 3.368, which is the 29.0 quoted throughout. The HWA model's unclipped forward (42.91) is kept but demoted everywhere to an explicitly labeled *diagnostic* (text, Table I column ">diag.", Fig. 3 legend) and described as "a diagnostic only, not a deployment option". The headline claim is reframed exactly as suggested: placement controls how fast the analog penalty grows. End-of-life penalty versus the pretrained model: 0.303 nats (sensitivity-aware) versus 0.389 / 0.447 / 0.448 (hardware_only / random / sequential), from a nominal (zero-tile-noise) floor of 0.082 nats. Title, abstract, contributions, Sec. IV-A, Table I (new Δ29 column) and the conclusion carry the new framing. The 42.91 line is still used as a secondary threshold in three places (IV-A "75/75", IV-C adversarial, IV-D sweep), each time labeled diagnostic; IV-D additionally now reports the sweep's end-of-life penalty versus the pretrained model (0.29–0.36 nats). The IV-A sentence about the baselines is now exact: random and sequential cross the diagnostic at end of life, hardware_only does in 6/15 conditions.

**T2 (Tables I and II disagree; aggregation unstated).** Accepted. The two numbers are different time aggregations, now stated in both places: the abstract says "averaged over the simulated lifetime" and the table caption says "per trace (5 timesteps × 3 realizations)". The end-of-life paired effect was computed exactly from the per-condition artifacts and promoted into the abstract and Sec. IV-B: **+0.085 nats, 95% CI [0.044, 0.127]** versus hardware_only (+0.143 / +0.145 versus random / sequential), consistent with your ln(42.82/39.30) ≈ 0.086 estimate.

**T3 (HWA training noise range may not cover the worst deployment noise).** Accepted, and now quantified in the paper rather than asserted. Regenerating the five Phase-2 traces (bit-identical to the archived ones): at map time every tile lies inside the U(0, 2σ_ref) training envelope (maximum 1.70–1.80 σ_ref across traces), but drift and faults push 5–12 of the 96 tiles above 2σ_ref by end of life, peaking at 3.1σ_ref over the five traces (the simulator caps tile noise at 0.080 = 3.5σ_ref; no trace reaches the cap). Sec. II-B now says the deployment is evaluated beyond its training envelope. More importantly, Sec. IV-B reports what each policy does with that capacity, computed from the archived Phase-3 placements (whose Φ values we reproduced to 1e-15): at end of life sensitivity-aware placement leaves 1.0% of total shard importance on tiles above the envelope, versus 5.2% for hardware_only, 9.6–10.2% for random/sequential and 33% for the adversarial bound; its importance-weighted tile noise at end of life is 1.06σ_ref versus 1.30 and 1.38–1.39. This is the mechanism behind "ages better" and it answers the comment directly: under the proposed policy the important shards essentially never leave the training envelope.

**T4 ("97.8% of the achievable headroom" overstates what the clairvoyant bounds).** Accepted: the text now says "97.8% of the headroom available from trajectory knowledge", names the span explicitly (random-to-clairvoyant; 96.3% when measured from hardware_only; 99.3% of the adversarial-to-clairvoyant span), and states in the same sentence that headroom from a better importance model lies outside all three spans.

**T5 (profile measured one projection at a time, deployed with 49 analog).** Agreed that this is the most consequential assumption. It is now stated where the profile is defined (Sec. II-C: "additive by construction … an assumption Section V revisits") and in the limitations. We implemented the check you proposed: `experiments/phase1_sensitivity/run_leave_one_out.py` (with `src/profilers/leave_one_out.py`) measures s_loo(p) = E[NLL(all 49 analog, noisy) − NLL(all analog, noisy, p restored)] on the all-analog model with exactly the Phase-1 noise fields (same seeds, same antithetic pairs, same σ_ref), in two restore modes (p analog but noise-free, which isolates p's noise exactly as Eq. (1) does; or p swapped back to its floating-point module), and reports Spearman ρ, Kendall τ and top-k overlap against the isolated profile. It is committed, wired into the Colab notebook (Cell 8h), documented, and unit-tested (the noise seeding, sign handling and restore bookkeeping were checked against the Phase-1 profiler with an exact numerical-equivalence test on a small model). It costs about one Phase-1 run (501 calibration passes on a GPU), which we could not complete before this revision; the paper therefore says "implemented in the released code but not yet run", and we will report the rank agreement as soon as the run finishes (before camera-ready, or in a follow-up note to you). Two mitigating facts: the end-to-end results are an implicit validation (a policy built on the additive profile lands statistically at the clairvoyant reference), and the ordering survives a ~100× magnitude shift between the pretrained and HWA models, suggesting rank stability.

**T10 (tile versus tier).** Added. Tiers are defined at first use (Sec. II-D: vertically stacked 512×512 crossbars sharing a tile's periphery); all 8 tiers of a tile share σ_i(t); "the quality map offers 96 distinct values for 678 placement decisions, so the mapper consumes per-tile write-verify statistics, not per-crossbar characterization", framed, as you suggested, as a point in the method's favour.

**T13 (the 90 spare tiers confound the random and sequential comparisons).** Partly dissolves: "random over the best 678 tiers" is exactly hardware_only (it sorts slots by quietness and randomizes shard order). The paper now says so ("equivalently, random placement restricted to the quietest 678 tiers"), which makes explicit that the headline comparison is capacity-controlled; the abstract quotes only the hardware_only gap, and the random/sequential gaps remain in Table II as gaps to what toolchains actually emit.

**T16 (σ_ref = 0.023 and "≈4-bit effective precision").** Definition added: 0.023·R_p equals the RMS quantization error (Δ/√12) of a ≈3.7-bit uniform quantizer over the programmed range; that is the sense in which we quote ≈4 bits.

**T17 (notation collisions).** Antithetic index renamed to a (realizations keep r); the placement map π is introduced in Sec. II-E and used as σ_π(s)(t); the weight notation is unified to W^c. (The materialization equation is now inline in Sec. III for space; its content is unchanged.)

**T18 (five phases, four labelled).** Section III is now titled "Phase 4: Lifetime Evaluation Protocol".

**T19 (two different quantities both 0.063).** Genuine coincidence (0.0628 and 0.0631 before rounding); the margin is now reported as ≈2.6 PPL so the two cannot be conflated.

**T23 (scale-generality claim points the wrong way).** Softened to a hypothesis with the risk direction you identified (LM-head parameter share ≈30% at 124M → a few percent at multi-billion scale, so the concentration may weaken); the "structurally representative" sentence is gone, and the prepared GPT-2 Medium configuration is named as the first test.

**Hybrid baseline (overall assessment, second issue).** You are right that the paper never compares against keeping the one or two dominant projections digital. We have not run that baseline for this revision; the limitations paragraph now names it explicitly ("no hybrid baseline: the LM head kept digital is the natural next comparison"), and it is cheap to add in our pipeline (two extra Phase-4 policies on the same paired noise). We would rather run it than argue it away, and will include it in the follow-up note alongside the leave-one-out result if you think it belongs in this letter.
